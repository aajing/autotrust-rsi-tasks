| Evaluation | Fixed evaluation protocol | Four families: F1 target pick/place among visually similar distractors; F2 precision placement into a narrow tray; F3 carrying a selected object around fixed tall obstacles; F4 placing two specified objects into separate trays in a required order. Every family appears at both hard and harder tiers in exploration, development and evaluation. Hard uses four objects, partial visual occlusion and one-step observation delay; harder uses six objects, additional similarly colored distractors and two-step delay. The renderer partially overlays one camera at a time and leaves the other free of that overlay. CPU checks cover observation structure and scene reachability; they do not establish learned visual recognizability or measured task difficulty.<br>F2 permits final horizontal center error of at most 6 mm in hard and 3 mm in harder. F3 obstacles extend above the maximum carry height so going around is necessary; the full carried-object swept volume must avoid them. F4 requires the first object to settle correctly before the second is grasped. Non-target objects must not move more than 15 mm from their initial positions in any family. Freeze 16 exploration, 6 development and 6 evaluation scenes per family/tier cell: 128/48/48 episodes. Use disjoint seeds and reject duplicate canonical geometries across splits. Public evaluation scenes vary poses, floor-grid appearance, brightness and camera occluders within the same declared ranges, not new task types.<br>Each episode allows 120 steps and 240 model calls. Interpolate each displacement over 0.5 simulated seconds at 0.002-second kinematic integration ticks; settling counts toward the 90-second simulation cap. The 120-step cap is tighter and yields at most 60 simulated seconds. Collision samples are at most 0.277 mm apart; this is sampled checking, not a continuous-geometry proof. All 224 scenes now have successful public full-state reference replays, including collision-free goal trajectories, recorded in public certificate JSONL. Canonical-geometry SHA-256 partitions assign train buckets 0–5, development 6–7 and evaluation 8–9; independently namespaced HMAC seeds and duplicate rejection prevent overlap. Every source, seed and certificate may enter the research workspace for inspection; executable controller input stays observation-only. These splits separate roles and geometries, not access privileges. Missing eligible scenes fail authoring without reducing counts. |

| Evaluation | Reward or score definition | Score = 100 × mean episode success across eight equally weighted family/tier cells; with six episodes per cell this is 100 × successful episodes / 48. Success requires every requested object fully released into its designated goal region, required order satisfied, and all goals stable for the final 0.5 simulated seconds, within step, safety and cost limits. F2 additionally meets its center-error tolerance. Full marks are exactly 100 only if all 48 episodes succeed. Ordinary missed grasps, wrong placements, safety collisions and step exhaustion terminate that episode with 0 while the remaining suite continues. Invalid actions or interfaces, cost/scope violations, process crashes, infrastructure failures, global timeout or an incomplete suite are unscored. No partial-credit geometric shaping changes the final reward. |
