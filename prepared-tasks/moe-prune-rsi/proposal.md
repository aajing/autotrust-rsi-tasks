| Research Question | Scientific question | How much permanent expert redundancy can an autonomous researcher remove from a multimodal decision model without losing its broader generative competence? The agent may discover expert importance, choose retained expert sets, adjust surviving router rows and perform bounded recovery. Evaluation covers free mathematical outputs, executable code/SQL, structured tool use, visual numeric/spatial answers and a smaller calibration component. The central question is whether pruning choices inferred from accessible examples transfer to new examples of the same families, especially across text and images. |
| Research Question | Why this is scientific research rather than pure engineering optimization | Routing frequency alone may confuse rare useful experts with redundant ones. The agent must investigate functional sensitivity, coverage across modalities and the effect of restricted recovery, under a fixed expert-removal budget. An ablation against frequency-only pruning tests whether its selection rule adds value. Quantization is excluded, so success reflects structural redundancy rather than a changed numeric format. Fixed top-eight routing means fewer resident experts need not reduce per-token work; the primary benefit measured here is removed expert capacity and weight footprint, with latency protected separately. |
